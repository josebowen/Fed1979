# Fed1979 verified primary sources (read 2026-11-11 from federalreserve.gov and FRASER PDFs)

## Sept 18, 1979 FOMC (fomcropa19790918.pdf, fomcmoa19790918.pdf, FOMC19790918meeting.pdf)
- Directive: funds rate initially to about 11-1/2%, range 11-1/4 to 11-3/4%. M1 3-8%, M2 6.5-10.5% (Sept-Oct).
- FOR (8): Volcker, Kimbrel, Mayo, Partee, Schultz, Teeters, Wallich, Timlen (alternate for NY).
- AGAINST (4): Balles, Black, Coldwell (wanted tighter: directive allowed too rapid money growth before triggering a rate rise; wanted upper limit 12%); Rice (wanted no firming: would intensify weakness, unlikely to affect inflation within 6-9 months).
- Quotes (transcript):
  - Volcker: "the decisions don't get any easier from meeting to meeting" / "There is a very strong possibility of recession on the one side." / "we have inflation as strong as ever. We have a difficult timing problem."
  - Rice: "whatever tightening of policy might be adopted today would be largely symbolic." / "I would want to stand pat."
  - Wallich: "we face basically a question of timing."
  - Coldwell: "If there is anything certain in this world right now it seems to be that inflation will continue"
  - Black: "dealing with inflation means getting a hand on the aggregates."
  - Teeters: "We have really gone very far, very fast in the past 6 to 8 weeks. I think it's time that we slow down and see what we are doing"
  - Balles (SF bankers): "very little evidence that the high level of interest rates is having any significant total effect on cutting off credit demand."

## Sept 18, 1979 Board discount rate (1979 Annual Report, FRASER arfr_1979.pdf, Board Policy Actions)
- 10-1/2 to 11%. FOR: Volcker, Schultz, Wallich, Coldwell. AGAINST: Partee, Teeters, Rice ("preferred a steady policy course for the immediate future").
- Partee and Teeters voted FOR the FOMC directive the same day and AGAINST the discount rate.
- Oct 6: discount 11 to 12% approved unanimously; 8% marginal reserve requirement.

## Oct 6, 1979 FOMC (fomcropa19791006.pdf, FOMC19791006meeting.pdf)
- New procedure: reserve aggregates; funds range 11-1/2 to 15-1/2%.
- FOR (12, unanimous): Volcker, Balles, Black, Coldwell, Kimbrel, Mayo, Partee, Rice, Schultz, Teeters, Wallich, Timlen.
- Quotes (Volcker):
  - "I think the rumors that were floating around in the market yesterday--first that I had resigned and then that I had died and then that I was mad at Governor Schultz--are symptomatic of the state of the market."
  - "Scylla and Charybdis have now come together" (Volcker attributing to Schultz)
  - "There is clearly no risk-free course for us here; there are risks on both sides."
  - "we can't walk away today without a program that is strong in fact and perceived as strong"
  - "It's an easier political sale" (about the new technique) / "more bang for the buck"
  - Administration "ready for a strong program" but "uneasy feeling about a shift in technique"
  - "there is no apparent encore for the Federal Reserve; we will have in effect shot our bolt."
  - risk of "the uncertainties of the money demand function"
  - "this kind of situation where there is no good answer"
- Partee: "a run from currency--a desire to get into goods and out of money"
- Rice: wanted wider funds range "9 percent on the low side and 15 to 16 percent on the high side."
- Wallich (to Rice): "all over the world they would be saying the new procedure really is a form of easing."
- Coldwell: "I'm not going to dissent ... I must admit that I think the M1 target is too high."

## June 30-July 1, 1982 FOMC (fomcropa19820701.pdf, FOMC19820701meeting.pdf)
- Unemployment 9.5% (May). M1 Jun-Sep ~5%, M2 ~9%; "Somewhat more rapid growth would be acceptable" given liquidity demands; funds 10-15%.
- FOR (8): Volcker, Solomon, Balles, Gramley, Martin, Partee, Rice, Keehn (alt for Horn).
- AGAINST (4): Black, Ford, Wallich (wanted M1 back in range; risk to expectations); Teeters (wanted clear signal of easing).
- Quotes:
  - Volcker: "I go back to the very first lesson of central banking, which is that in a crisis you lend freely." / forcing rates down "may backfire because it won't look sustainable to the market." / credit controls, usury ceilings "symptomatic of the time."
  - Teeters: "I want to get interest rates down." / "to avoid, really, almost a catastrophe" / "I think we need a signal in the market that we have eased or rates won't come down"
  - Wallich: "I don't think this is the time to make a fundamental change in policy." / "I would bet on the rise in velocity."
  - Black: "it would preserve our credibility by coming in at the top of our target"
  - Ford: "I certainly am not a fan of high interest rates, but I very strongly oppose any shift in policy toward putting on a maximum rate cap"
  - Gramley: "a very frightening situation" (fiscal paralysis)

## Oct 5, 1982 FOMC (fomcropa19821005.pdf, FOMC19821005meeting.pdf)
- M1 distorted by maturing All Savers certificates and new MMDA; FOR (9): Volcker, Solomon, Balles, Gramley, Martin, Partee, Rice, Teeters, Wallich. AGAINST (3): Black, Ford, Horn.
- Martin: "the first thoughts will be that we caved in, the election is coming, and here they go again."
- Roos (nonvoting): "To ignore M1 would really run the flag up that in some people's perception we are making a basic change."
